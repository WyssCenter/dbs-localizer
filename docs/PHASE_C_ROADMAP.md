First run to pass the gate on a real subject, in 4 s (release build).

Every contact lands in dense metal. The same subject through the *previous* tracer put them at
14–31 HU, the 72nd percentile — soft tissue. Coordinates are `head` `(−21.9, −8.1, −9.0)` and
`(22.9, −8.1, −10.5)`, spans exactly 9.00 mm, which is GPi-region and bilaterally symmetric.

Two things this run established that the phantom could not:

- **The comb model of CORE is wrong for real CT.** A normalised post-op CT samples the lead as
  one continuous plateau, not four resolvable contacts, because blooming exceeds the 1.5 mm
  insulator gaps. Correlating a contact comb against a plateau peaks where the comb straddles
  the plateau's *edge*, which put contact 0 nearly a full span too deep. CORE measures the
  metal's distal edge and length instead.
- **The extent check has to be one-sided.** The lead *body* above the contacts is radio-opaque
  and reaches past the top of the trace window, so requiring an observed proximal edge refused
  the subject outright. Only a region that is too *short* is disqualifying.

**Not verified: absolute accuracy on real data.** There is no expert reconstruction on disk —
that is the deferred OSF Tutor pull — and the intensity-based checks above confirm the contacts
are *on metal*, not that they are on the right millimetre. Attempts to derive ground truth from
the CT alone failed: a PCA fit to the >2000 HU voxels inside a brain-sized box has 6.7 mm radial
spread, because the skull is comparably bright in this normalised volume.
